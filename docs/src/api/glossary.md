# Glossary and Acronyms

[B](@ref) | [C](@ref) | [E](@ref) | [F](@ref) | [I](@ref) | [K](@ref) | [N](@ref) | [O](@ref) | [P](@ref) | [R](@ref) | [S](@ref) | [T](@ref) | [W](@ref)

### B

  - *Base year*: The year that all costs in the objective function are discounted to and
    expressed in. Defined in the `Portfolio`.

### C

  - *Capacity expansion*: The problem of deciding which generation, storage, and
    transmission capacity to build, and when, to meet demand at the lowest cost.
  - *Capacity factor*: The fraction of the installed capacity of a technology that is
    available in a time step.
  - *Capital recovery factor*: The factor that converts an overnight capital cost into an
    equal yearly payment over a capital recovery period. See
    [Cost Accounting](@ref cost_accounting).
  - *Co-located technology*: A wind plant, a solar plant, and a storage device that share
    an inverter and a connection to the grid.
  - *Cumulative capacity*: The capacity of a technology installed up to and including an
    investment period: its existing capacity plus everything built so far.

### E

  - *Energy share requirement*: A policy that requires the energy generated by a set of
    technologies to be at least a fraction of the demand, such as a renewable portfolio
    standard.
  - *Expression*: A linear combination of variables and constants that is stored in the
    optimization container so that it can be reused in constraints and read in the results.

### F

  - *Feasibility period*: A period used to test an investment plan without adding
    operation costs to the objective function.
  - *Formulation*: A set of variables, constraints, and cost terms that represents one
    aspect of a technology or requirement. See [Formulations](@ref formulation_axes).

### I

  - *Investment period*: A span of dates in which capacity can be built. Build variables
    have one value per investment period.

### K

  - *Key*: The identifier of a variable, constraint, or expression in the optimization
    container and in the results, made of its type, a technology type, and a formulation
    name. See [How a Model is Built](@ref build_process).

### N

  - *Natural units*: Physical units, such as MW and MWh, as opposed to per-unit values.
    All quantities in `PowerSystemsInvestments.jl` are in natural units.

### O

  - *Operational period*: A stretch of consecutive time steps, typically a day, that
    represents the operation of the system during an investment period. Also called a
    representative period or representative day.
  - *Optimization container*: The object that holds the `JuMP` model and the variables,
    constraints, and expressions added to it.
  - *Overnight cost*: The cost of building a unit of capacity as if it were paid in full at
    the time of construction, without financing costs.

### P

  - *Portfolio*: The data of a planning problem: technologies, regions, requirements, time
    series, and financial assumptions. Defined in
    `PowerSystemsInvestmentsPortfolios.jl`.
  - *PSINV*: `PowerSystemsInvestments.jl`
  - *PSIP*: `PowerSystemsInvestmentsPortfolios.jl`
  - *PSY*: `PowerSystems.jl`

### R

  - *Region*: A location in which supply and demand are balanced: a `Zone` or a `Node`.
  - *Requirement*: A policy that constrains several technologies together.

### S

  - *Solution algorithm*: The method used to build and solve an investment model, such as
    solving it as a single optimization problem.

### T

  - *Technology*: Something that can be built or that takes part in the energy balance: a
    supply, storage, demand, or transport technology.
  - *Template*: The modeling choices of an investment model. See
    [`InvestmentModelTemplate`](@ref).
  - *Time step*: One interval of an operational period, typically an hour.
  - *Transport technology*: A technology that moves energy between two regions.

### W

  - *WACC*: Weighted average cost of capital. The rate of return required by the mix of
    debt and equity that finances a technology.
  - *Weight*: The number of real periods that an operational period stands for. Operation
    costs and energy in an operational period are multiplied by its weight.
